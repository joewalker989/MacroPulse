# Rights and professional review workbook

All approval fields start **pending**. A worksheet is not a release, contract or
professional signature. Keep the actual agreements and review evidence in a private,
access-controlled location. Publish only agreed credits and stable evidence references.

## 1. Owner/producer rights clearance

For each master and delivery file, record:

- Asset ID, version, filename and SHA-256; creator/copyright owner and provenance.
- Demonstrator and voice-performer consent evidence, with an authorized representative
  where relevant. Use adult participants for this pilot. Confirm permission to use the
  performance, likeness and voice in MacroPulse; ownership of the camera file alone
  does not establish those permissions.
- Written scope covering commercial in-app/web use, distribution channels, hosting/
  streaming, territories, term, permitted technical edits, captions, coaching and
  illustrative muscle graphics. Confirm any retained branding, credits and restrictions.
  Offline redistribution is a separate scope item; this player currently does not offer it.
- Clearance of location, visible third-party content and incidental sound where needed.
- Whether the right holder can actually grant the rights; resolution of contradictory
  terms or uncertainties by the owner and suitable legal adviser before approval.
- Required public attribution and license URL; receipt/evidence ID, approver and date.

For existing wger material, retain Goulart and its per-record source/license. CC BY-SA
4.0 covers its specified copyright permissions, includes attribution/adaptation terms,
and does not generally license third-party personality rights. Do not invent a model
release from a CC label. Consult the [actual legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
and keep any separate permission. No need to publish a private model agreement or infer
that the entire app becomes the media's license merely because it streams a clip.

### Ready-to-send rights enquiry — owner sends after checking recipient and terms

“We are preparing human exercise demonstrations for MacroPulse, a fitness app. Please
confirm who controls the copyright and whether you can authorize the proposed app/web
streaming and distribution. We need written terms covering the selected files, credit,
territories, duration, technical encoding, captions, optional voice coaching and muscle
schematics, plus appropriate demonstrator/voice and location permissions. Please identify
restrictions, cost, and any consent/clearance evidence you can provide. We will not label
the footage professionally reviewed without a separate review of the final files.”

No enquiry has been sent and no purchase or supplier agreement has been accepted.

## 2. Reviewer appointment

Date verified by owner: __________________  Scope accepted: ________________________

Select someone qualified to assess the specific strength movements and explain the
anatomy. A relevant accredited personal-training/strength credential or physiotherapy
qualification is a starting point, not automatic proof of competence for every clip.
The owner verifies the credential and scope. Decline the assignment if outside scope.
Research references in the brief support preparation; they are not provider endorsement,
permission to reuse their media, or a substitute for watching the final footage.

We need your review of final delivery files, camera labels, complete-rep timestamps,

Please state your relevant qualifications, scope, fee and evidence format; identify
required reshoots or corrections. Approval must name the exact file hashes. Please do
not approve placeholders or imply that the player assesses the user's own form.”

Muscle graphics are region schematics, not measured activation or a detailed anatomical
overlay. If a label cannot be represented accurately by the current zones, omit it or
request a separately reviewed graphic change; do not relabel shoulders as arms or claim
precise muscle activation from motion. Explain primary/secondary roles in reviewed text.

## 4. Final media inspection and handoff

Technical reviewer records final codec, pixel format, dimensions, fps, duration, bytes,
fast-start status and hash; checks no cuts inside approved reps and no generated frames.
Rights and content approvals must refer to the same delivery hash; the coaching review
also names the voice hash. A re-encode changes the hash and needs delivery-copy review.

Private evidence IDs can refer to signed PDFs or a documented review session with the
reviewer's explicit approval. Stable access-controlled HTTPS links are acceptable;
expiring signed URLs, passwords, tokens and private documents do not belong in GitHub.
No signatures or approvals may be supplied by the developer on someone else's behalf.
